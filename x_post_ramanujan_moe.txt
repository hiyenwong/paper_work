I've been thinking about how MoE layers handle expert interaction. Current practice: each expert routes tokens independently, but there's no structure for experts to exchange information. As model scale hits thousands of experts, this creates a communication bottleneck.

A natural question: what's the theoretically optimal sparse graph for connecting N experts under a fixed degree d?

Enter Ramanujan graphs — expanders with the best possible spectral gap:

λ ≤ 2√(d-1)

They achieve provably near-optimal information mixing:

1. Any d-sparse expert graph has a Ω(log_d N) lower bound for global information propagation.
2. Ramanujan graphs hit O(log N) mixing time — essentially optimal.
3. For MoE layers constrained to d interactions per expert, this gives a near-optimal propagation schedule.

This is a *topology design*, not a training acceleration. The contribution is structural: a provably efficient expert interaction backbone.
